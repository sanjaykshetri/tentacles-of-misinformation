6  Chapter 4: Spotting Pseudo-Profound Bullshit — A Gentle NLP Project

6.1 What Is “Pseudo-Profundity”?

Some statements sound wise but mean nothing:

“The growth of deep evolutionary forms brings about the creation of the ever-present dynamic world that radiates in all directions.”

vs.

“Societies that preserve social bonds through shared rituals experience more cooperation.”

One is nonsensical. One is meaningful. But both can feel profound.

My thesis included a Bullshit Receptivity scale (BSR-10) measuring susceptibility to this linguistic deception. This chapter uses NLP to build a classifier that detects pseudo-profound language.

6.2 Dataset & Definitions

6.2.1 Real Data

  • Collection of pseudo-profound statements (from research literature)
  • Collection of mundane but true statements
  • Target: Binary classification (profound-sounding vs. grounded)

6.2.2 Linguistic Hypotheses

Pseudo-profound language may be characterized by: - N-gram patterns: Unusual word combinations that lack semantic grounding - Vocabulary: Overuse of abstract nouns, psychological jargon, vague modifiers - Structure: Complex syntax masking logical vacuity - Sentiment: High positive valence (sounds “inspiring”)

6.3 NLP Pipeline

6.3.1 1. Tokenization & Preprocessing

  • Lowercase, lemmatization, stop-word handling
  • Preserve linguistic richness (don’t over-process)

6.3.2 2. Feature Extraction

6.3.2.1 TF-IDF Baselines

Quick, interpretable, surprisingly effective.

6.3.2.2 Embeddings (SBERT)

Capture semantic meaning using transformer models.

6.3.2.3 Linguistic Features

  • Flesch-Kincaid readability
  • Average word length
  • Syntactic complexity (parse trees)
  • Emotional tone (NRC lexicon)

6.3.3 3. Classification

  • Logistic Regression (baseline)
  • SVM (robust to high-dimensional embeddings)
  • Random Forest (nonlinear patterns)
  • Fine-tuned BERT (if sufficient data)

6.4 Results & Interpretation

Key findings: - Which linguistic signals most predict pseudo-profundity? - Can humans and machines agree on what’s bullshit? - Limitations: Context matters. Is “metaphor” the same as “bullshit”?

Confusion matrix and ROC curves included.

6.5 Critical Reflection

Important caveat: This is NOT a “truth detector.”

Pseudo-profundity is about linguistic form, not factual accuracy. A true statement can be expressed beautifully. A false statement can be precise.

The real utility: - Flag suspiciously complex language that may hide weak thinking - Raise cognitive friction: “Pause and ask: does this actually make sense?” - Combine with other signals (source credibility, evidence backing) for better assessment
